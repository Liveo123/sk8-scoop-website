HALLOWEEN HEADLINER

GRIMMFEST 2026

8–11 October · ODEON Great Northern, Manchester

Illustration: SK8 Scoop · created for this feature.

If your preferred level of Halloween is less “friendly pumpkin” and more “someone should probably not go into that basement”, Grimmfest is the big one this weekend.

Manchester’s international festival of fantastic film runs for four days with horror, thrillers, sci-fi and dark comedy, alongside Q&As, signings and a Saturday/Sunday market.

One important bit before you set off: day and weekend passes have sold out. Grimmfest’s 5 October survival guide says individual tickets are still available for most screenings, so those are the useful route now.

Films start at the advertised time with no commercials beforehand. Most screenings are rated 15 or 18, so check the individual certificate before booking.

BEFORE YOU GO: The main Great Northern Car Park 2 beneath the ODEON is currently closed. Grimmfest says Car Park 1 remains open, and the main shutter entrance to the cinema is also unavailable, so use the Deansgate Mews doors.
SCARE-O-METER 4/4Proper horror. Check the individual film certificate.

LOCAL HALLOWEEN

BRAMHALL HALLOWEEN EVENT & MARKET

Illustration: SK8 Scoop · a seasonal impression, not a photograph of the 2026 market.

Saturday 10 October · 10am–3pm · Bramhall Village Square, SK7 1AW

If you want Halloween without going full haunted-house mode, Bramhall is the gentler option this Saturday.

Worker Bee Markets is filling the pedestrianised Village Square with up to 50 hand-picked makers and traders from 10am–3pm. Stallholders will be dressing up in spooky costumes and decorating their stalls, so this one should feel more Halloween-y than a normal market day.

For families, the organiser has now confirmed a fruit-and-veg carving competition and a children’s costume competition, plus face-painting, slime-making and close-up magic. At least a quarter of the makers are described as hyper-local, including people from Cheadle Hulme, Heald Green and Cheadle.

The surrounding independent shops, cafés, bars and restaurants are open as well, so this works as a proper wander-and-lunch afternoon rather than an event you have to build the whole day around.

SCARE-O-METER 1/4Mostly pumpkins, treats and market browsing.

Trail daily · 10:30am–5pm
Scarecrow-making this weekend · 11am–1pm and 2pm–4pm

Quarry Bank’s first Scarecrow Festival is already running, and this weekend has an extra reason to go.

On Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October, the Scarecrow Field has drop-in make-your-own-scarecrow sessions. No workshop booking is needed.

The Sally Scarecrow Trail costs £2.50 per person. Normal Quarry Bank admission still applies, and National Trust members get normal property entry free but still pay for the trail.

It is outdoor-led, so this is one for decent shoes and whatever coat October has decided to demand.

THE PLACE THAT VANISHED

HEYHEAD: THE VILLAGE THAT DISAPPEARED

SK8 Scoop editorial reconstruction and schematic · NOT TO SCALE · generated historical scenes are illustrative, not archive photographs.

There used to be a small village just over a mile south-west of Heald Green station.

Heyhead was not folklore. Historical records describe a real hamlet of farms and market gardens, with a Congregational chapel documented from 1862, shops, homes and local families.

Then two expanding worlds began closing in.

Manchester’s suburbs grew from the north. The airport expanded from the south.

Manchester archive material records farming and market-gardening families in the area, alongside compulsory purchase of local land. By 1992, surviving Heyhead buildings were being photographed shortly before the main early-1990s clearance connected with airport expansion and parking.

The story needs one correction to the neat version: Heyhead did not vanish in one dramatic afternoon, and the airport was not the only force reshaping it. Different properties disappeared at different times as Manchester and Ringway changed around the hamlet.

NO GHOSTS REQUIRED: The real story is strange enough.

My reaction is mostly disbelief at how completely a place can vanish. Heyhead wasn’t an ancient settlement lost centuries ago: it was a small farming and market-gardening community just over a mile from Heald Green station, with 267 residents recorded in 1871. As Manchester expanded northwards and the airport grew beside it, Heyhead was squeezed out.

FOOD & DRINK · CHEADLE

IF YOUR HALLOWEEN ENDS IN A PINT: THE WHITE HART

Halloween illustration: SK8 Scoop · sign, lamppost and REDRUM clue retained without the clipped top artefact.

Editorial illustration: SK8 Scoop, based on the White Hart exterior reference image supplied for this issue.

90 High Street, Cheadle, SK8 1PX

The White Hart is a proper Cheadle all-rounder rather than a special Halloween event: a historic Greene King pub on the High Street with a beer garden, car park, dog-friendly and family-friendly facilities, and live sport.

Food is classic pub territory — burgers, pies, fish and chips, small plates and vegetarian and vegan choices — so it works just as well for something to eat as it does for a quick pint after wandering around the decorated streets.

This is actually my go to pub in SK8. It always has a nice atmosphere with lots of people enjoying themselves, it is cozy, has a pretty nice beer garden and the food is good. I always seem to find myself there if I go out for a quick one in Cheadle.

FREE IN HEALD GREEN

A BREW, A CHAT & A BIT OF KNITTING

Monday 12 October · 1–2pm · Heald Green Library · free

Halloween illustration: SK8 Scoop · not a photograph of the library session.

Forget haunted houses. The real Halloween terror is noticing a dropped stitch when you were feeling quite pleased with yourself.

This free drop-in at Heald Green Library is a gentler sort of spooky. Bring your knitting, crochet or another craft, grab a brew and a chat, and spend an hour with other local makers on Monday 12 October.

It’s open to beginners and experienced crafters, so you do not need to arrive with advanced scarf-based credentials. Just bring something to work on and enjoy a pleasantly unchaotic hour — unless somebody starts knitting eyeballs again.
