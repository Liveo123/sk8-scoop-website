Stockport station could change. Here's what the plans actually mean.

An accessible bridge, new station buildings and better connections between Edgeley and the town centre are among the proposals for Stockport railway station. There's still time to comment.

SK8 Scoop · 10 November 2026 · Public consultation closes 11.59pm, Thursday 19 November 2026

The important bit: The plans are at consultation stage. None of these improvements is a finished or guaranteed project. You can examine the options and submit comments online until 11.59pm on Thursday 19 November.

What's being proposed?

The plans describe changes to the buildings and approaches on both sides of the tracks, along with a new footbridge with lifts and stairs. The intention is to improve movement between the station, Edgeley, the town centre and nearby transport connections.

For people who already use the station, the practical question isn't just what looks good on a visualisation. It's how the entrances, bridge and lifts would work on the journey you actually make.

Why should Cheadle Hulme and SK8 readers care?

Stockport is a key rail interchange for people arriving from Cheadle Hulme and making onward journeys. If you change trains at Stockport, arrive with a buggy or luggage, or need step-free access, the proposed layout could affect how you move through the station and surrounding streets.

Equally, the proposals deserve practical scrutiny: the consultation is a chance to explain what could make connections easier, and what looks difficult or unclear.

Useful questions to consider

  • Would the proposed bridge and lifts make changing between platforms and reaching the surrounding streets easier?
  • Are the new entrances and routes clear for passengers arriving from Edgeley or the town centre?
  • What would make access safer and less confusing with a wheelchair, pram or suitcase?
  • Does the suggested layout work for your own commute or occasional journey?

How to respond

Open the official consultation, review the plans and complete the feedback form before the deadline. You don't have to agree or disagree with everything. Specific examples from your journeys are likely to be more helpful than a vague endorsement or objection.

Read the plans and have your say

These are proposals, not approved construction works or guaranteed outcomes. The council's consultation is the authoritative source for the latest documents and deadlines.
