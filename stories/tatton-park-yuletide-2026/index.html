Yuletide at Tatton Park: is it worth booking?

A folklore-filled light trail with a surprisingly practical question at its heart: which date gives you the outing you want at a price that makes sense?

Last checked14 NOV 2026Part of SK8 Scoop Issue 21's website update.
At a glance · 2026 season

What the ticket includes

Yuletide at Tatton Park combines an outdoor folklore-themed illuminated trail with live performance, music, storytelling and seasonal characters. It takes place in the gardens of Tatton Park, Knutsford. Sessions run on selected dates from 14 November to 23 December 2026, not every day.

Paul's verdict from Issue 21: “There’s fire juggling too (for some reason, I love fire juggling - Paul).” The practical point is to choose your timed entry before travelling.

Dates and tickets

Which days can you book?

Remaining November dates: Saturday 21, Sunday 22, Saturday 28 and Sunday 29 November. December: Saturday 5, Sunday 6, Saturday 12, Sunday 13, Saturday 19, Sunday 20, Monday 21, Tuesday 22 and Wednesday 23 December. The event organiser lists entry slots from 3pm; its current ticket page gives a 7.30pm last-slot range, while an information page says 7pm, so trust the available slot at checkout rather than arriving without a ticket.

Off-peak 21–22 November: adults £19.50, children £11.50. Peak from 28 November: adults £23, children £14. The organiser lists children aged two and under as free. Two paying adults and two paying children cost £62 off-peak or £74 peak in admission, excluding any extra spending or fees. Availability and prices must be reconfirmed at checkout.

Before you go

Parking, footwear and access

The organiser instructs drivers to enter Tatton Park through the Rostherne entrance (sat nav WA16 6SG), not simply follow a generic park postcode. Parking during the event is free from 2pm, with an optional £1.50 environmental donation.

This is mostly outdoors. Wear suitable footwear and warm layers. The organiser's different 2026 pages disagree on trail length: the ticket page refers to roughly 1km, while its information page says 1.4km. Expect a substantial outdoor walk rather than relying on either number as exact.

Some sections are not fully wheelchair accessible; the organiser says alternative routes and assistance may be available. Check its access guidance before booking for particular mobility or sensory needs. Plan around a suggested 90-minute visit, but the time you spend can vary.

A decision worth making beforehand

Is the family ticket cost worthwhile?

For a family with two adults and two paying children, the quoted standard ticket costs change by £12 between the named off-peak and peak dates. Add travel, food or optional treats before choosing a date. If you need a lower-cost outing, a local free event may be a better fit; that is what the SK8 free-events finder is for.

After 23 December 2026, treat this article as a record of the season rather than a current booking guide.
