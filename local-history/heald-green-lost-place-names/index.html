The old place names still hiding in modern Heald Green

Long Lane, Hurlbote and a field called Near Long Flatt are fragments of an older local map that survived after the landscape around them changed.

Checked2 OCT 2026Adapted from SK8 Scoop Issue 15 and checked against Heald Green Heritage.
Three names to notice

The map changed. Some of the names did not.

Long Lane · Hurlbote · Near Long Flatt
Long LaneAn old local area name

Heald Green Heritage describes Long Lane as running along what is now Wilmslow Road, from around Schools Hill south towards the Waggon and Horses.

HurlboteA place-name that faded

Hurlbote was recorded as a hamlet and as a turnpike toll-point name. Unlike Long Lane, it has largely vanished from everyday use.

Near Long FlattAn 1839 field name

The old field name survives in historical records around Finney Lane. “Flatt” was used for cultivated ground.

Look at the ordinary names

The road name outlived the place it described

Long Lane was not a formal political boundary. Heald Green Heritage describes it as an older local area running along what is now Wilmslow Road, from around Schools Hill south towards the Waggon and Horses. Most of that area is now simply understood as part of Heald Green, but the name still turns up in local memory and in the Long Lane Post Office name.

That is what makes these names useful. They are not just labels. They are evidence that a different version of the neighbourhood once sat underneath the one we use now.

The older landscape

Hurlbote and Near Long Flatt

Historic local records around Finney Lane include a field called Near Long Flatt in 1839. The word “flatt” was used for cultivated ground. Nearby, Hurlbote appears as the name of a hamlet and turnpike toll point, but the name has almost disappeared from the modern local map.

That contrast is useful in itself: some names cling on for generations while others vanish almost completely.

Why it is worth knowing

Modern road signs can be scraps of an older map

The point is not to memorise obscure names. It is that ordinary addresses, road signs and business names can preserve pieces of a landscape that no longer looks anything like the one that created them.

The interesting bit for me is that the name just carries on. Houses arrive, roads widen and shops change, while a small piece of the older place survives in plain sight.
