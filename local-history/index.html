What the local history source actually says

Heald Green Heritage records that the Dutton family divided their bungalow near the hotel and rented one half to Jack and Marthe McKenna. It identifies Marthe as the Belgian nurse and First World War spy whose memoir became I Was a Spy.

The Gibson link is a locally recorded account

Heald Green Heritage says Tony Townsend, the hotel licensees’ son, confirmed that Guy Gibson and around 40 RAF men used the hotel’s upstairs Committee Room during the war. That is interesting evidence, but it should stay labelled as a local recorded account rather than silently becoming a primary-source fact.

The story gets better when the labels stay honest

Primary or official evidence should carry hard facts. Local research and recollections can add valuable colour. Anything unresolved stays visibly unresolved, even when a neater version would make a punchier anecdote.
