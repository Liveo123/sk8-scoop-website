The eight civilian names on Cheadle War Memorial

The rear plaque does not only remember servicemen. Eight names record civilians killed in two fatal air raids on ordinary Cheadle streets in 1940 and 1941.

Checked18 SEP 2026Based on Historic England and the verified local casualty/history research used for SK8 Scoop Issue 13.

The detail that changes the memorial

Eight names tell a different kind of wartime story

Cheadle War Memorial was originally built for the First World War, commemorating 101 men from Cheadle. After the Second World War another plaque was added on the rear. Historic England records 54 Second World War casualties, and at the bottom is a separate heading: AIR RAID VICTIMS.

Those eight names came from two fatal raids on Cheadle. The useful local-history detail is that the victims can be connected to streets and addresses that still exist now.

The Christmas raid

Bulkeley Road, night of 23/24 December 1940

During the Manchester Christmas Blitz, a 1,000lb bomb came down on Bulkeley Road. Historic England says the German bomber was thought to have been targeting a factory making aircraft wings. That remains a theory, not an established fact.

Local memorial and casualty research places the main impact at 49 Bulkeley Road. Seventeen-year-old Ronald Frederick Harrison was caught in the passage between Nos. 47 and 49 and later died at Stockport Infirmary. Nora Jackson, at No. 51, was also killed. A resident at No. 20, opposite, was fatally injured.

At 33 Wilmslow Road, John Thomas Lydiatt, who had a shoe-repair shop there, died after finding an incendiary bomb behind the premises.

There is a source wrinkle. Historic England dates the first raid to 24 December, while individual casualty records use 23 December. The safest description is the night of 23/24 December 1940.

The February raid

Stockport Road by Boundary Bridge, 4 February 1941

The second fatal raid came at about 10pm on 4 February 1941. Historic England records a lone bomber hitting a house on Stockport Road. Local historical research places the bomb at the rear of 245 Stockport Road, by Boundary Bridge.

Agnes Bennison, 62, and her daughter Kathleen, 27, were killed in the house. Two local fire watchers were also caught nearby. Robert Campbell was killed, while Fred Miller was badly injured and died the following day at Stockport Infirmary.

A later local historical account notes two factories close to the railway bridge and main road and says the bomb missed them by about 100 yards. Whether the bomber was actually aiming for them is not known, so that is context rather than certainty.

Why this belongs online

The addresses make the history feel local again

Together, the two raids account for the eight civilian air-raid victims recorded on the memorial. They were not killed serving somewhere distant. They died on ordinary Cheadle streets.

SK8 Scoop has kept the source disagreement and uncertainty visible rather than forcing the story into a tidier version than the evidence supports.
