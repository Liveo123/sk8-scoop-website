The lost buildings beside Stockport’s rivers

An archive doorway without an agreed explanation, a riverbank of former workshops and the remains of a mill, housing and gasworks beneath Heaton Lane.

The enormous doorway that raises more questions than answers

One old Stockport riverbank photograph shows what appears to be a doorway around nine metres high, with possible steps cut into the rock. The archive information reviewed for Issue 22 does not establish its exact location or purpose. We are not presenting a theory as a discovery.

Elsewhere along the rivers, foundries, market routes and bridges once formed a much more industrial landscape than visitors see today. The names Millgate Bridge and Lancashire Bridge preserve parts of a complicated story of crossings and redevelopment, although the precise location of the mysterious doorway remains unresolved.

What archaeology actually found at Heaton Lane

Excavation ahead of work at Heaton Lane identified traces of Wellington Bridge Mill and its associated machinery, as well as the remains of back-to-back and terraced workers’ housing. Later, the gasworks expanded into much of the housing area. By the twentieth century the site had seen further demolition and became a tram and bus depot, then a car park.

This is not guesswork based on a postcard. The Wessex Archaeology excavation assessment documents the sequence, supported by historic maps and physical structures. It also records uncertainty in dating individual remains.
