Heald Green · local history

The spy next door and the future Dambuster upstairs

Heald Green Hotel / Finney Lane · two wars, one ordinary-looking corner and a building now facing another change.

There is a stretch of Finney Lane where two national wartime stories ended up almost on top of each other. The hotel had opened only in 1936. A few years later, a celebrated First World War spy was living next door while RAF airmen were bedding down upstairs.

That neighbour was Marthe Cnockaert McKenna, a Belgian nurse who secretly worked for British intelligence under the codename Laura. Her cover was unusually convincing because it was also real: she genuinely treated wounded German soldiers and was awarded the Iron Cross for her nursing. At the same time she was gathering information for the Allies, using the access her nursing work gave her to German military personnel. Her later memoir also described sabotage work, although its more cinematic details are best treated cautiously.

She was caught in 1916 and imprisoned until the Armistice. In 1932 she published I Was a Spy!, with a foreword by Winston Churchill, and a year later Victor Saville turned it into a film starring Madeleine Carroll. Her story had reached British cinemas before she settled in Heald Green.

Heald Green Heritage records that Marthe and her husband Jack McKenna later lived in half of the large bungalow beside the hotel, while Jack was involved with the NAAFI operation at nearby Ringway. The local account says Marthe continued writing there.

Then the next war arrived almost at her front door. Ringway Airport opened in 1938 and quickly became an important wartime aviation centre. Heald Green Heritage, drawing on local wartime research and memoir-linked accounts, says the hotel’s upstairs committee room was used by around 40 RAF men, including Guy Gibson.

This was before Gibson became commander of 617 Squadron and led the Dambusters raid in 1943. The accommodation was not exactly officer-club glamour. The local account describes men sleeping on straw-filled palliasses on the floor, sharing one bathroom, and being taken between the hotel and Ringway by bus. The RAF uniforms attracted attention in the bar, with locals sometimes buying the young airmen drinks.

So, within a few yards, you had a German-decorated British spy and bestselling author living beside a hotel being used by a young RAF pilot who would later become one of the best-known airmen of the Second World War. Heald Green was not the centre of either story. For a short time, though, this ordinary bit of Finney Lane was where they overlapped.

And there is a reason to tell it now. Manchester City Council is currently considering planning application 144554/FO/2025, which includes demolition of the existing hotel building as part of a redevelopment proposal. The physical place where these two stories intersected may itself be about to change again.

Source caution

The Guy Gibson connection

The RAF accommodation and Gibson detail is retained with attribution to local historical source material. It should not be read as saying that 617 Squadron or the Dambusters operation was based at the hotel.
