Why the frog is called Mercury

The answer starts with an old telephone exchange, takes a small detour through the periodic table and ends with a giant frog on Finney Lane.

Checked17 SEP 2026Telephone history checked against Heald Green Heritage; frog history checked against One Stockport records.
The short version

MERcury became part of Heald Green’s local vocabulary.

Why MER?Heald Green meets chemistry

Local telephony research records that HEA and CHE were already in use elsewhere. MERcury gave Heald Green its own exchange name, with Hg doubling as the chemical symbol for mercury and an abbreviation of Heald Green.

2021Mercury Frog arrives

Heald Green’s One Stockport frog was named Mercury. Contemporary local records say the name came from the area’s former telephone identity.

Before all-number dialling

Your telephone number could begin with a word

For much of the twentieth century, British telephone exchanges were commonly identified by names. The letters around a rotary dial turned the first part of that name into the digits needed to route a call.

Heald Green did not initially have its own exchange. Heald Green Heritage’s research records newspaper references to a new exchange on Finney Lane, with cable work under way in 1958. An article dated 18 September 1959 announced that the MERcury exchange would begin operations, and a December report covered its official opening.

The oddly neat name

Why Mercury?

Heald Green Heritage records the explanation from local telephony researcher Graham Whiteside: three-letter exchange codes had to avoid names already in use. HEA was already associated with Heaton Moor and CHE with Cheetham Hill, while Cheadle had been served through GATley.

The chosen name was MERcury. It came with a pleasing local coincidence: Hg is the chemical symbol for mercury and also works as an abbreviation for Heald Green.

That is why Mercury keeps turning up in local names and memories. It was not invented for the frog decades later. The frog inherited an older Heald Green identity.

Fast-forward to 2021

Then somebody made it amphibious

Stockport’s 2021 frog project placed giant sculptures in district centres as well as the town centre. Heald Green residents and businesses helped fund their own frog, which was named Mercury.

One Stockport and local fundraising reports say the frog came to Finney Lane, carried imagery of local landmarks and took its name from the old telephone identity. The wider frog project also raised money for St Ann’s Hospice.

So the giant frog is not a random bit of street furniture with an unexpectedly scientific name. It is a rather large reminder of the era when saying “Mercury” helped tell the telephone system where in south Manchester you were trying to ring.

One useful correction

MER did not simply become 637 for local numbers

It is tempting to translate MER directly on a dial and assume all Heald Green numbers therefore started 637. Heald Green Heritage specifically notes that local memories and later numbering do not support such a simple rule. The exchange-name history is real; the exact journey from named exchanges to later all-number formats was more complicated.
