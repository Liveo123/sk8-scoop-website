What lies beneath Cheadle Green?

Next time you cross Cheadle Green, consider what stood there before the lawns. The village once had Cheadle Hall, and archaeological work has uncovered evidence of the buildings that occupied this spot.

The dig on the Green

In September 2010, as part of the celebrations marking two centuries of Cheadle Green, archaeologists investigated the site of the former hall. The work involved the University of Salford's Centre for Applied Archaeology and the South Manchester Archaeological Research Team, working with local civic organisations and Stockport Council.

The excavation sought to understand the surviving remains and development of the hall. Its story runs through the 18th and 19th centuries, and the hall itself was eventually demolished. The remains are not an open-air ruin or visitor attraction today.

An older surprise

Further investigation nearby uncovered evidence of an earlier timber-framed structure beneath later cottages and stables. Finds included medieval pottery and decorative tiles, evidence that takes the local story well beyond the hall most recently remembered on the site.

Look, don't dig. There is no public archaeological excavation to explore. The interesting thing is knowing that a seemingly ordinary stretch of village green has several layers of history underfoot.
