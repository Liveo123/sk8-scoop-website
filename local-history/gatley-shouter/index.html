Local history case file · Gatley

The Gatley Shouter

A ghost story, a Victorian glossary entry and a large stone that nobody can now confidently point to. The useful bit is separating what was actually written down from what the folklore says happened.

Checked17 SEP 2026Folklore is labelled as folklore. Documentary clues stay separate.
The 30-second version

Three layers of the Shouter story.

Document · tale · unresolved
Documented phrase“Shaouter o’ Gatley” existed by 1886

Robert Holland’s Glossary of Words used in the County of Chester records the expression for a loud or boisterous person. That predates Fletcher Moss’s published ghost versions.

Victorian folkloreFletcher Moss wrote down the ghost story

Moss published versions in 1894 and 1898. The later account identifies the Shouter as Jim Barrow and places the tale around Carr Lane, Gatley and Northenden.

Still unresolvedThe man and the stone are not securely identified

The tale says the spirit was finally put beneath a large stone near a parson’s gate. SK8 Scoop has not found evidence that proves which surviving stone, if any, was meant.

Before the ghost story

The name was already local

The strongest little clue comes from 1886. Robert Holland’s Cheshire glossary includes “SHAOUTER O’ GATLEY” and explains that a loud-spoken, boisterous person was formerly called one. It is labelled to the Wilmslow area.

That matters because it shows the expression was in circulation before Fletcher Moss published his fuller supernatural versions in the 1890s. The Gatley Shouter was not simply a phrase invented for a later ghost book.

The folklore

Moss’s noisy spirit

Fletcher Moss wrote down two versions of the story, in 1894 and 1898. In the later version the Shouter is described as the ghost of Jim Barrow, linked in the tale with Cross Acres. The spirit is said to travel from the Northenden side towards Gatley and to haunt the route around Carr Lane and Gatley Carrs.

Its repeated confession is wonderfully specific: “Milk and water sold I ever, Weight or measure gave I never.” The story presents Barrow as a dishonest trader being punished after death for watered milk and short measure.

The finale gets stranger. A parson confronts the spirit with local people carrying Bibles and praying. The Shouter becomes smaller and is eventually said to be confined beneath a large stone near the parson’s gate.

The awkwardly useful bit

It may also be a travelling folk tale

Very similar stories about dishonest milk sellers appear elsewhere in English folklore, including versions with almost the same confession. That leaves a good possibility that an older tale was localised over time with Gatley geography, Carr Lane, Cross Acres and the name Jim Barrow.

That does not make the Gatley version less interesting. It makes it more interesting in a different way: a travelling story may have picked up enough local detail to become part of the area’s own folklore.

What remains unproven

No plaque for the stone just yet

SK8 Scoop’s original Issue 10 research found a real James Barrow who died in Gatley in 1873, but that alone does not establish that he was Moss’s Jim Barrow, that he sold milk, or that he lived at Cross Acres.

Likewise, Moss writes about a large stone as though his audience could understand the reference, but we have not found evidence securely identifying a surviving stone as the Shouter’s Stone. Anyone pointing at a convenient rock with great confidence still owes us the paperwork.
