Local history · Cheadle

The pub that used to be a station

The Cheshire Line Tavern is not railway-themed décor in an old-looking building. It is the surviving station building from Cheadle’s former railway station.

The building hiding in plain sight

You can still read the railway in the pub

The next time you meet somebody at the Cheshire Line Tavern on Manchester Road, you are meeting them in part of Cheadle’s old railway station. The long building beside the former line and the pub’s name are not a later nod to railway history. The station building itself survived.

The architectural record dates the station to 1865–66 and gives its opening date as 1 February 1866. The main brick building stood on the Liverpool platform, with a separate brick shelter for passengers travelling towards Stockport.

Where could you go?

Local station, much bigger railway reach

Cheadle was served mainly by local trains running from Stockport Tiviot Dale towards Liverpool Central, with many services terminating at Warrington Central. Express trains also passed through from the station’s early years.

Then came an especially good railway-nerd detail. In 1902, the Liverpool Curve opened 64 chains east of the station, roughly 1.3 kilometres. It gave direct access to the Midland Railway main line to London, putting this modest Cheadle station on a route with a much wider reach than the surviving setting suggests.

The end of the trains

Cheadle became Cheadle North, then closed

The station was renamed Cheadle North on 1 July 1950. Passenger services ended on 30 November 1964.

The useful twist is what did not happen next. The main station building was not lost. It survived, was converted into a pub and restaurant, and is now the Cheshire Line Tavern.

Why keep this online?

It changes an ordinary trip down Manchester Road

This is exactly the sort of local history that works better as a permanent searchable page than a one-week newsletter item. Once you know what the building was, its shape and position make considerably more sense.

If your family remembers Cheadle North in railway use, or has a photograph of the station before its pub years, SK8 Scoop would be interested in hearing from you.
