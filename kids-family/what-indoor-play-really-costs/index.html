Home / Kids & Family / Indoor play costs

Family life · 26 November 2026

It's raining. The kids are climbing the furniture. What will soft play actually cost?

The headline child ticket doesn't always tell you what a rainy-day outing will cost.

It's raining. The children are climbing the sofa, and you're calculating how much longer the sofa can take it. Soft play seems like the obvious answer. Until you start comparing prices.

Take two children, aged two and five, and one adult. At Wacky Warehouse in East Didsbury, the published standard admission works out at £12. At Head Over Heels in Stanley Green, one official booking page puts a 90-minute visit at £34.15 (another official price page lists £29.88). That's before anybody orders a coffee.

Of course, these aren't identical experiences. A larger venue can offer a whole afternoon of adventure, while a smaller one might be exactly right for an hour. But which places give you the most for your money, which suit a toddler and an older sibling, and where can you actually sit down?

We've pulled together 15 indoor play options around SK8, Stockport and just beyond, including a farm play barn, a miniature role-play village and a climbing centre for older children. The most useful discovery isn't a single winner: it's how much the right answer depends on your family.

The quick answer

Use our age, area and example-budget filters to compare 15 venues. There's no signup wall.

Find your indoor play option

1. A free adult can make a big difference

Tumble Jacks in Marple lists £8.50 for a two-year-old and £10 for a five-year-old. Adults go free, and you can stay during open hours without a timed booking. The example total is £18.50. Let Loose in Hazel Grove lists £8.60, £9.70 and £1.95 respectively: £20.25 for a 90-minute weekend session. Think about time, not just the sticker price.

2. The venue your toddler wants may not be a soft-play centre

The Place to Play in Reddish has over 12 themed role-play areas. Curious Little Ones in Adlington has a separate pre-walker space and more open-ended play; a child-and-adult ticket plus a sibling aged one or older comes to £17.90. Pure Imagination in Poynton is another option for children who would rather invent stories than race down slides.

3. What about the ones who've outgrown the ball pit?

Awesome Walls in Bredbury offers instructor sessions from age six. Jump Heaven in Hazel Grove advertises an hour of jumping for £15 per child online; if two children need new compulsory grip socks, that's another £6. The supervision rules matter just as much as the price.

4. Before you leave the house

Look at the full family bill, how long you can stay, whether a session must be booked, and where you can park. Ask about access or sensory needs rather than relying on a generic accessibility label. Tumble Jacks advertises dedicated SEN sessions with reduced lighting and no music; Oxygen in Handforth advertises a Peaceful Play session for ages four and up.

And if you are staying close to home, don't overlook Nixon's Play Barn in Heald Green. The outdoor garden is closed in winter, but the indoor barn has bouncy castles, frames and soft play. Confirm the booking price; its published booking wording is inconsistent.
