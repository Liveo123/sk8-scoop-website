What is going up at Cheadle Eco Park?

If the big timber structure on Bird Hall Lane has caught your eye, it is a six-unit light-industrial business park with some unusually ambitious environmental targets.

Build statusUNDER WAYCurrent completion target: March 2027
The 30-second version

What the large wooden frame is becoming.

Bird Hall Lane · Cheadle
01 · SiteSeven-acre business park

Stockport Council says the former industrial site is being redeveloped into six new light-industrial units of different sizes.

Useful distinction: BREEAM Outstanding and the employment figures are project targets/expectations. They are not presented here as guarantees about the completed park.

What is being built?

Six units for light-industrial businesses

Cheadle Eco Park is being built on a seven-acre site on Bird Hall Lane that previously held older industrial buildings. Stockport Council says the completed scheme will provide six new units for businesses needing space to grow.

The council expects the park to support around 200 jobs once occupied. That number is an expectation tied to the project rather than a count of jobs already created.

Why all the timber?

The structure is part of the environmental plan

The most visible feature is the engineered timber frame. Stockport Council says the project is using a structural frame made entirely from sustainably sourced timber rather than a conventional steel frame.

The council’s July 2026 update says the timber choice, combined with other sustainably sourced materials, is delivering a 40% reduction in embodied carbon. The development also includes air-source heat pumps, natural ventilation, energy-efficient lighting, rooftop solar panels, electric-vehicle charging and sustainable drainage measures.

The scheme is targeting BREEAM Outstanding. That wording matters: it is the standard the project is aiming for, not a rating SK8 Scoop is claiming has already been awarded to the finished buildings.

What happens next?

The frame is only the conspicuous middle bit

The latest council update, published 10 July 2026, showed construction progressing and the timber frame going up. The site is also due to gain new landscaping, trees, a rain garden and walking and cycling links.

The current published completion target is March 2027. As with any live construction project, that is worth rechecking if you are reading this much later.
