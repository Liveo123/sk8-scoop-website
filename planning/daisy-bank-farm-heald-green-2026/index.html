Daisy Bank Farm: what could change, and what deserves protecting

A farm linked to Heald Green's early settlement is the subject of new housing proposals. That is not the same as permission to demolish the buildings.

Last checked14 NOV 2026Part of SK8 Scoop Issue 21's website update.
A place with a longer memory

What is Daisy Bank Farm, and why does it matter?

The Styal Road end of Finney Lane was part of Heald Green before the railway transformed the area. In research published on 4 November 2026, local historian Helen Morgan traced Daisy Bank Farm through old maps, farming families and memories of the fields around it. The research cites local historian Joan Heinekey's estimate that the farmhouse dates to around 1750; it is a reported estimate, not a confirmed construction date.

Much of this history sits beneath ordinary modern street names. The stories are about families, stables and yards as much as buildings: an agricultural community that helped make the village, rather than a picturesque isolated farmhouse.

The farming connection

Two different farms, one easily confused history

Our Issue 21 newsletter made an important mistake. It said cattle crossing Simonsway came from Daisy Bank Farm. They came from Chamber Hall Farm. Daisy Bank is a separate site on Finney Lane. Heald Green Heritage pointed out the error, and our Saturday resend carried an apology.

The connection between the farms is real but different: Heald Green Heritage says Daisy Bank's land and buildings were farmed as part of the neighbouring, larger Chamber Hall enterprise. Its account describes dairy activity and the later adaptation of outbuildings.

Read our correction and the Chamber Hall cattle memories.

The development question

Does a housing allocation mean the farm will be demolished?

No. A housing allocation is not a demolition permission. The September 2026 draft Stockport Local Plan includes a Daisy Bank allocation of approximately 132 homes, marked HOM 2.41. A published analysis by Heald Green Ratepayers says the proposed site is about 2.83 hectares east of Daisy Bank Lane.

Importantly, the Ratepayers' analysis says the draft policy requires complete retention of Daisy Bank Farm, which the council has identified as a non-designated heritage asset. The group also reports that a developer held a separate pre-application consultation covering approximately 137 homes. Neither figure is an approved permission, and the two consultation processes should not be confused.

Heald Green Heritage has raised concern for the site, particularly about the future of the old buildings. That concern deserves reporting. It would be inaccurate, however, to present demolition as an agreed outcome.

What happens next?

Three things worth watching

  1. The Local Plan: whether the heritage-retention requirement remains in the adopted policy.
  2. Any detailed application: the actual boundary, proposed treatment of the farmhouse and outbuildings, and supporting heritage documents.
  3. The buildings themselves: how retention would work in practice, including repair, ongoing use and the surrounding setting.

As of this update, we have not established an approved planning application authorising demolition. That is a material distinction, not a small print caveat.
