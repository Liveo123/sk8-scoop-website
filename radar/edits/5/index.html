Two Things Not to Miss

The pool Withington saved

Sat 28 Nov, 10.30–11.30am · Withington Baths, 30 Burton Road · £6.13 online · ages 12+

AI illustration: imagined historic pool interior, not a photograph of Withington Baths.

On Saturday, a volunteer guide takes you behind the scenes of a pool that opened in 1913. The Baths says it became the first in Manchester to offer public mixed swimming a year later. A community-led charity saved it from closure in 2015, and it’s still a working pool.

The £5 ticket (£6.13 online) includes a mince pie and a hot drink. Places are limited and advance booking is recommended. Under-16s must come with an adult.

Look Again

Three secrets of Didsbury

Three free daylight detours, each with one detail to look for.

The Didsbury gate that once cost £10

Any daylight visit · Stenner Lane, Didsbury · free

Look up at the Eagle Gate by the Old Parsonage. Fletcher Moss bought it for £10 when the Spread Eagle pub on Corporation Street was being demolished. The Parsonage Trust records another £80 to put it up.

Read the story of the Eagle Gate

The mathematician hidden on a railway bridge

Any daylight visit · East Didsbury railway bridge, Tesco side · free

Look for Dame Kathleen Ollerenshaw among the painted figures. A leading expert on magic number lattices, she also served as Manchester’s Lord Mayor in 1975–76. The Civic Society’s guide also picks out an aviator and an engineer among the portraits.

Find Dame Kathleen on the mural guide

The mother who gave Didsbury a garden

Any daylight visit · entrances on Palatine Road and Dene Road, West Didsbury · free

Marie Louise Silkenstadt died just three years after she married. Her mother, Josephine, bought land off Palatine Road to create a garden in her memory. Her father had come from Bremen and made his fortune in cotton. The opening ceremony took place at the Dene Road entrance in June 1903.

The Culture Radar

Spectrum 4: four artists in a former parsonage

Until Fri 18 Dec · Old Parsonage, Stenner Lane, Didsbury · free

Cate Gibson, Janet Higgins, Anne Mackinnon and Carolyn Murphy have exhibited together for more than a decade. Their Steve Parle Room show puts original printmaking, pastels and paintings side by side. See what each medium brings to the landscapes and abstracts.

The Parsonage advises checking ahead if you’re making a special trip, as rooms can be in use during the week. A grotto shares the site on Sunday 29 November. Check gallery access if visiting that day.

See the exhibition details

Magic Place: become part of a digital funhouse

Opens Sat 28 Nov · Manchester Museum, Oxford Road · £5 standard, £3 concessions, under-fours free

Move across the exhibition hall and a colourful silhouette moves with you. Genetic Moo’s installation changes its images and sound as you move. Try standing still, then take a few steps and see what happens to your shape. Adults can look a little silly here too. There are quieter sessions with fewer tickets available. Book a timed slot. The installation runs until 17 January, and the museum says you can stay beyond your entry slot once inside.

South Korean band Leenalchi takes pansori, a tradition of sung storytelling, into a room full of bass, funk and new-wave rhythms. Listen to those voices and bass lines push against one another on Here Comes That Crow.

Step inside a Victorian Christmas

Sun 29 Nov, Wed 2 and Thu 3 Dec · 84 Plymouth Grove · timed entry, £9.50 standard

The drawing room at Elizabeth Gaskell’s House (not dressed for Christmas) · Michael D Beckwith · CC0 1.0

Elizabeth Gaskell’s own rooms have been dressed for a Victorian Christmas. You can sit on the chairs and touch many of the objects. That makes the visit feel unusually intimate for the home of a writer who paid such close attention to domestic life. Book a timed visit. The house is open 11am–4.30pm on those days, with last entry at 3pm. Children aged 12 and under go free with a paying adult.

Film · The Quiet Girl

AI illustration inspired by the film’s themes; not a film still or a depiction of its cast.

Colm Bairéad’s Irish-language drama follows a quiet child who spends a summer with relatives in rural Ireland. Pay attention to what the adults do around her. A meal, a chore and a little patience can say more than anyone manages out loud. BFI Player lists the film with English subtitles. Check the current viewing options.
