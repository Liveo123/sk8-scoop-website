Gatley Carrs: an easy walk through the wild bit behind Gatley

Nature reserve, brook, meadow and ponds · verified external route/map.

One of the nicest things about Gatley Carrs is how quickly suburbia seems to fall away. The conservation group describes 19 acres of woodland, scrubland and grassland, plus four acres of willow swamp and two ponds, with paths running through the woods, meadow and alongside the brook.

Route at a glance

Easy · 2.9 miles / 4.7km · about 1hr 25min · circular

The verified Ramblers route includes Gatley Carrs Nature Reserve and Walter Stansby Memorial Gardens. Its published start is St Luke’s Church in Wythenshawe, not Gatley village.

Open the Ramblers route

The Carrs is more interesting than “some woods behind the houses”. Its history page says the wet ground on the Mersey floodplain supported osier beds, with willow grown for basket-making. Farming faded in the 1920s, the site then spent decades as landfill from roughly 1930 to 1980, before being restored as public open space with trees, ponds and a wildflower meadow.

The conservation group says Gatley Carrs is a Local Nature Reserve, jointly managed with Stockport Council. It highlights woodland, willow carr, meadow, scrub, ponds and the watercourse.

Before you go

Check the route you actually want to follow.

The conservation group directs drivers via Brookside Road, SK8 4BG, lists free parking, and warns that the main car-park gate is locked at dusk. Its wheelchair/buggy wording applies to the Carrs itself, not automatically to every metre of the longer Ramblers circular.
