# SK8 Scoop NUE rules

These rules govern how the website should move readers from one useful experience to the next.

## Progressive novelty

When one SK8 Scoop page is a likely next step from another, the destination should reward the click with new value rather than repeating the same featured stories, images or editorial payload.

Practical rule:
- Homepage feature stories should not simply be repeated on Around SK8 or other likely next-step pages.
- Gateway pages should introduce different stories, angles, examples or deeper context.
- Repetition is acceptable only when continuity genuinely requires it, such as a persistent signup route, a critical live deadline, a core navigation cue or a factual reminder needed to understand the next step.
- A reader who follows a prominent internal link should normally encounter something meaningfully new within the first screen or first major content block.

Why this matters:
- it avoids making the site feel circular;
- it gives each page a distinct editorial purpose;
- it increases the reward for exploring further;
- it supports the NUE principle that every click should lead to a more useful next experience, not just another version of the same one.

## Existing design safeguards

- Do not use generic AI-style SVG icon tiles.
- Prefer distinctive editorial visuals, local photography, maps, diagrams, case boards and typographic treatments.
- Check important imagery for crop loss on desktop and mobile.
- Avoid unnecessary image repetition across pages.
- Keep pages useful before promotional.
- Use one primary next experience and no more than two secondary routes where practical.
- Do not invent live events, prices, planning facts, historical claims or other local facts.
