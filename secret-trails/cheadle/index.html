How it works

Download. Explore. Collect evidence. Solve the case.

The mechanics are deliberately simple. The fun should come from noticing real details, making connections and uncovering the local story, not from deciphering complicated instructions.

Inside the case file

A proper little case file, not a worksheet with a walk attached.

The download combines clue pages, side cases, a route overview, backup clues and a final deduction. The preview below is deliberately spoiler-safe.

What children actually do

Different jobs at different stops.

Children look closely, turn what they see into evidence, unlock a little local story, then keep moving. It is intentionally not five versions of “find a number”.

The route

Six Cheadle stops, with Abney Hall as the finale.

The trail starts at St Mary’s, moves through the village and ends at Abney Hall. The image is a decorative schematic rather than turn-by-turn navigation.

Before you go

Very little kit. Quite a lot of looking.

Use the PDF on a phone or print it, bring a pen or pencil if useful, wear sensible shoes and have a grown-up handle crossings and route decisions.

Ages 7–9Likely to need more help with reading, interpreting clues and keeping the evidence organised.
Ages 10–12Probably the easiest range for letting children lead most of the solving themselves.
Ages 13–14Give them the case file and let them run the investigation, with the adult mainly handling the route.
A proper finish

There is a final deduction, then a clear case-closed moment.

The earlier evidence matters again at the end. Families make the final connection at Abney Hall, close the case and can finish with an optional detective photo challenge.

Public beta

Try it, spot the rough edges, tell us what happened.

The useful feedback is very practical: a clue that is hard to see, an instruction that makes no sense, an awkward route moment, or whether your child simply got bored. That is what will improve the next version.

Most useful: children’s ages, approximate time taken, the clue that caused most trouble, anything unsafe or difficult to see, and whether your child would do another trail.

Quick questions

Before you head out.

Do I need to print it?

No. You can use the PDF on a phone. Printing is handy if children want to write directly on the case file.

Does it cost anything?

No. The public beta is free to download and the trail itself does not require paid admission.

What if a clue cannot be seen?

Use the backup clue in the PDF and continue. Please tell us afterwards so we can improve the next version.

How long should I allow?

Allow around an hour for now. Actual family timings are part of the public-beta test, so some groups may be quicker or slower.

Is it a guided event?

No. It is a self-guided family trail. A grown-up remains responsible for road crossings, route choices and normal family safety.

Why is it a beta?

The desk research and build are complete enough to test, but local families still need to expose anything that has changed or is harder to see in real life.

Ready to take the case?

Download the PDF, head to Cheadle and see whether your family can uncover the hidden name and make the final connection. No extra signup is required.
